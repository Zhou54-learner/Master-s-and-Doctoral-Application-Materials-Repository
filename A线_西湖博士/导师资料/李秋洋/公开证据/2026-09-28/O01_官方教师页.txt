Qiuyang Li, Ph.D. - Westlake University
Dr. Qiuyang Li was born in the city of Maanshan, Anhui Province. He received B.S. degree with honor of Materials Physics from University of Science and Technology of China in 2013. He received Ph.D. degree in Chemistry from Emory University (USA) in 2019 under the supervision of Prof. Tianquan (Tim) Lian, using transient absorption spectroscopy to study excitons in 2D nanocrystals. After graduation, Dr. Qiuyang Li joined Department of Chemistry, Columbia University (USA) as a MRSEC Postdoctoral Fellow working with Prof. Xiaoyang Zhu on angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy and 2D materials. Later, he moved to Department of Physics, University of Michigan (USA) working with Prof. Hui Deng on light-matter interaction and cavity engineering of 2D materials. Dr. Qiuyang Li is an assistant professor at the School of Engineering, Westlake University, leading an independent research group to explore ultrafast spectroscopic research and cavity engineering of low-dimensional quantum materials.

Dr. Qiuyang Li uses static and ultrafast spectroscopy (including but not limited to transient absorption/photoluminescence spectroscopy, angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy, time-resolved Faraday rotation spectroscopy, confocal microscopy, etc.) to explore and manipulate photophysics, emerging properties, and new states of matter of low-dimensional quantum materials. He has published 47 papers in total, including 20 first-authored papers in top journals like Phys. Rev. Lett., Nature Communications, J. Am. Chem. Soc., ACS Nano, Nano Lett., Chem. Rev., and Acc. Chem. Res.
Li Research Group focuses on interdisciplinary research involving ultrafast spectroscopy, condensed matter physics, and cavity engineering. The main research directions include: (1) cavity sensing and engineering of 2D moiré quantum matter; (2) ultrafast optical control and cavity engineering of collective behaviors and ordered states in low-dimensional quantum materials; (3) establishing, probing, and engineering of newly formed 2D van der Waals multiferroic materials.
