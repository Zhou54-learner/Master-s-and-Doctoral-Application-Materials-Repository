The interplay among lattice, orbital, spin, and charge degrees of freedom in quantum materials gives rise to a rich landscape of emergent electronic phases, including unconventional magnetism, ferroelectricity, charge and spin density waves, superconductivity, etc. These correlated electronic states exhibit rich and exotic quantum phenomena at two-dimension, surfaces and interfaces, and confined systems.
Our research group combines a suite of complementary experimental techniques—including crystal growth, optical spectroscopy, transport, and scanning probe microscopy—together with theoretical approaches such as symmetry analysis and numerical simulations, to investigate the microscopic mechanisms underlying these correlated electronic states. The group further explores their responses to external stimuli, including electric fields, magnetic fields, doping, and strain, with the goal of achieving precise control over electronic properties and functionalities.
The unique physical properties of quantum materials provide exciting opportunities for next-generation information technologies, particularly in data storage and computing. These unconventional electronic phases may enable advanced functionalities such as ultrafast dynamics, high energy efficiency, robust endurance, highly sensitive readout, and compact integration. Our group designs novel device architectures, fabricates them at the nanoscale, and systematically evaluates their potential functionality.
