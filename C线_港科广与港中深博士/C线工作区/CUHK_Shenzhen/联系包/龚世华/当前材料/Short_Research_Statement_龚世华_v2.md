# Short Research Statement — Zijing Zhou

My interest in scientific computing grew from a concrete limitation of my undergraduate research. In my thesis on resistive-film microwave absorbers, I worked with a commercial frequency-domain electromagnetic solver through an existing Python–CST workflow, which I extended with additional design variables, geometric screening, and automated simulation and data-processing steps. When I compared thin-film and Ohmic Sheet representations, I saw that modelling choices could materially change the computed electromagnetic response and the resulting optimization outcome. This made me increasingly interested not only in obtaining a final design, but also in understanding the numerical models and solvers that produce the result.

I am particularly interested in frequency-domain Helmholtz and Maxwell problems because they connect directly to the electromagnetic simulations I have already performed while requiring the mathematical and numerical understanding I now want to develop. I would like to progress from application-level simulation toward finite-element formulations and reliable numerical methods for wave problems, learning how discretization, solver and preconditioning choices affect accuracy, robustness, and computational cost.

As a bachelor-entry applicant, my preparation is currently stronger in applied physics and simulation workflows than in numerical analysis. I see doctoral training as a transition rather than a restart: I can bring experience extending parameterized computational workflows, automating simulation runs, and examining how modelling choices affect results, while building the mathematical and programming foundations required for numerical-method research. I would prefer the specific research problem to be developed through discussion with the supervisor rather than fixed in advance.
