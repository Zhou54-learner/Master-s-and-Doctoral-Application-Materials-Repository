# Short Research Statement — Zijing Zhou

One question that emerged from my undergraduate research is how much a simulated electromagnetic response depends on modelling and numerical choices made before a solver returns a result. In my thesis on resistive-film microwave absorbers, I compared thin-film and Ohmic Sheet representations within a Python–CST workflow. The comparison showed me that different modelling choices could change the computed response and, in turn, the outcome of an optimization process. This made me realize that obtaining a numerical result is only part of the problem; understanding when and why that result is reliable is another research question in itself.

This is why I have become interested in frequency-domain Helmholtz and Maxwell problems. I would like to understand more clearly how model abstraction, discretization, solver design, and preconditioning influence accuracy, robustness, and computational cost in wave simulations. My present experience is mainly at the application level, so I am especially interested in learning how these ingredients are formulated and analysed rather than committing in advance to a particular numerical method or thesis topic.

Prof. Gong’s work on numerical methods for frequency-domain wave problems, including antenna electromagnetics, is closely related to the questions that grew out of my previous simulation work. I hope to begin by strengthening my understanding of finite-element formulations and numerical analysis for standard frequency-domain wave problems, and then refine a more specific research direction through discussion and training. My previous experience with parameterized simulations and workflow automation gives me a practical reference point for thinking about modelling reliability, while doctoral study would allow me to investigate these questions with stronger mathematical and numerical tools.
